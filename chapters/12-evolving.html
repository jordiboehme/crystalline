12  Evolving

When a colleague gets something wrong, you do not fire them. You say what was wrong, you point at the page that explains it, and the next time is better. Every team keeps its people’s knowledge honest that way, and it works because no one expects what a person learned in March to hold forever. Information lives. The cutoff changes, the vendor rewrites its terms, the customer’s CTO who decided in the room is replaced by one who reads. What you taught the agent in chapter 9 and handed it in chapter 10 was never set in stone. It was set in files, which is better, because a file can be corrected.

Evolving is the beat that does the correcting. Chapter 3 walked it once around; this chapter works it at the pace of a real archive.

12.1 Why knowledge needs tending

Nothing in an archive goes wrong dramatically. A validity window ends on a Tuesday and the engram still says stable on Wednesday. A review date on a runbook that names a person comes and goes. A replacement lands in one session and the session ends before the old engram was flipped. Somebody types a correction into Fluid on a Friday and nobody looks at it again.

Then the structural kind: a link that points at a title nobody captured, a live engram citing one that has retired, a supersedes written on one side only, an engram nothing links to, one that grew past the point where it should have split, one that never grew past its frontmatter, a reference to an attachment whose file is gone, a file in assets/ that no engram claims. And the redundant kind: two engrams saying nearly the same thing, two titles a link cannot tell apart, one tag spelled two ways. None of it is a mistake anyone made. It is what happens to knowledge when time passes, and it is what a colleague six months in will confidently repeat if nobody tends it.

12.2 The sweep

The agent asks for it with evolve_engrams, or you ask from a terminal with crystalline evolve to see what the agent would see. What comes back is a ranked queue and nothing else; the sweep is read-only, and the standing reminder at the bottom of every run says so. The queue is written for the agent to work: each finding names the engram, the evidence it fired on and the one action that clears it, with the tool that performs it. The three families are the paragraphs above in order, temporal, structure and redundancy; a run narrows to one family, one rule, one domain or a minimum priority, and --today evaluates the date rules as of a fixed day so a run reproduces. Tyrell’s engineering domain, the first Monday in October:

Sweep of engineering as of 2026-10-05
41 engrams scanned, 3 findings (showing 3, page 1)
temporal 1, structure 1, redundancy 1

1. [85] V001 JUDGMENT
   Broker migration freeze  crystalline://engineering/broker-migration-freeze
   validity window ended 2026-09-30 but the status is still stable
   evidence: valid_to=2026-09-30; today=2026-10-05; status=stable; inbound refs 1
   fix: set_frontmatter valid_to=<later date> or status=superseded

2. [50] V102 MECHANICAL
   Retry queue drain procedure  crystalline://engineering/runbooks/retry-queue-drain-procedure
   unresolved reference [[Edge health check]]
   evidence: rel_type=depends_on; nothing titled `Edge health check` in engineering; nearest is `Edge health checks`
   fix: [[Edge health check]] -> [[Edge health checks]]

3. [30] V203 JUDGMENT
   crystalline://engineering
   2 tag spellings look like one tag (plural variants)
   evidence: #runbook used 2 time(s); #runbooks used 9 time(s)
   fix: crystalline tags merge runbook runbooks

The number in brackets is the priority, how much the archive gains from clearing this one first; a finding on an engram that many others link to, or that carries a salience, is lifted, because a wrong hub does more damage than a wrong leaf. The word beside the rule id decides who acts.

12.3 Mechanical and judgment

A mechanical finding completes intent the archive already records. The link in item 2 points at a title that differs from a real one by a letter, and fixing it invents nothing, so the agent does the mechanical findings in one pass and reports once at the end, the way you would fix a colleague’s typo without a meeting about it.

A judgment finding would change what the archive claims, and that wants a yes first. Item 1 asks whether the migration freeze ended or was extended, which only a person who was there can say. The agent brings it to you as a proposal with the evidence and its own reading of it, and writes only when you say yes:

Agent:  The broker migration freeze ran to 30 September and the
        migration landed on the 12th. I read this as ended rather than
        extended: retire it, close the window on the 30th, and carry
        the "no payments deploys during a broker cutover" lesson into
        the deploy runbook? (y / n)

Some judgment findings carry a rule of their own. An engram a person captured directly in Fluid is judgment class whatever else is true of it: the agent verifies the claim, aligns the tags and wires relations both ways, but proposes any change to the wording and waits, because the words are the human’s (Chapter 9). And tag drift, item 3, comes back as a command rather than an edit, since folding a tag is a bulk rewrite the agent never does on its own: it hands you crystalline tags merge runbook runbooks and leaves it there. The merge rewrites the tag everywhere and records the fold under ## Tag Aliases in the manifest, so the old spelling keeps finding what it always found and the sweep never raises the pair again.

Then ask again. The second run re-derives the queue from scratch, nothing about the first was stored, so what is left is only what is still true. At Tyrell the three become one, the tag merge that waits for a terminal, and it is waiting on a person rather than on the archive. That shrinking number is the feedback loop.

In a domain that reviews changes before they land, chapter 13’s overlay mode, a sweep covers the reviewed folder plus the sweeper’s own drafts, so the problems in a draft are its author’s to see and nobody else’s, while a finding on the shared truth shows for everyone.

12.4 The page for people

Fluid’s maintenance page shows the same queue to a person (Figure 3.2): the stale dates, the half-finished retirements, the unreviewed human captures, ranked the same way, every finding a link to the engram it fired on. It is there for your curiosity and for a sense of how the knowledge is doing, and it is a report rather than a workbench, on purpose. The queue’s consumer is the agent, so no button on the page edits an engram. The work a finding names is the agent’s, done in the engram itself. What you do on the page is watch the list shrink. Its three buttons are about the queue rather than the knowledge: acknowledge a finding as intentional, take that back and delete an orphaned attachment. What an acknowledgment silences is never hidden: a toggle draws the silenced rows muted.

12.5 Retiring done right

The sweep finds retirements that were never finished, so it is worth being precise about finishing one, starting with the question chapter 3 asked: does the new fact make the old one false going forward? If the old fact was never quite true, a cutoff measured from the wrong event, it is corrected where it stands. If it was true and stopped being, the rule went away with the broker, it is superseded. Correction is a sharper measurement. Supersession is the world changing.

Supersession has four steps. The replacement is written as its own engram, status: stable, with - supersedes [[Old title]] among its relations. The old engram’s status is set to superseded and its valid_to to the real date the fact stopped holding, never a guess and never a placeholder, each an edit_engram call with set_frontmatter, one lifecycle field by name. The old engram gets - superseded_by [[New title]], so the graph reads the same from both ends. And any lesson worth keeping moves forward into the live engram as an observation, unbounded, because the lesson is still true even though the fact is not. Skip the second step and the sweep raises a half-finished retirement; skip the third and it raises a one-sided pair. Do all four and the sweep has nothing to say.

Not every retirement has a successor. A practice that was simply abandoned gets the status that fits, deprecated for do-not-do-this-again, archived for kept-for-the-record, legacy for still-true-of-the-old-installation, the window closed when the date is known and the lesson carried into a live engram.

The old engram does not fade out of the photograph the way Marty’s siblings did. It stays in every search, dated, softly below the current one, and the next time somebody asks why an old dashboard still says twenty-four hours it is the whole answer, with the date the answer changed.

NoteIn practice

In October Weyland-Yutani replaces its CTO. Vickers, who skimmed decks and decided in the room, leaves for a board seat, and Bishop, who reads everything sent in advance and decides nothing live, takes the chair. After the first meeting with him Deckard hands his agent the notes the way he handed it the first three: learn how this one decides. The agent derives the new pattern, materials in advance and a decision by email a day later, and then does something Deckard did not ask for. It recognizes that “Weyland-Yutani CTO: one-pagers, decides live” was true and has stopped being, and proposes a supersession rather than an edit: a new engram for Bishop’s pattern with supersedes pointing back, the old one flipped to superseded with a valid_to of the day Vickers left, and one lesson carried forward, unbounded: the pattern belongs to the person, so learn the new decision-maker before the first meeting. Deckard says yes. The Q2 notes stay attached to the retired engram, still its provenance.

A month later the sweep raises the settlement-batch engram in clients, which carries a relates_to of its own back to the Vickers engram: a live reference to retired knowledge. The agent proposes repointing it at the Bishop engram. Deckard says no: the batch was scoped in one of Vickers’ meetings, and the citation is history rather than guidance. The agent acknowledges the finding on the engram with a note, “lineage citation, keep”, and the sweep stops raising it while the citation and the retirement both stand.

12.6 Verification and acknowledgments

Some findings are answered with a field rather than an edit, and telling the two fields apart keeps the archive honest. A re-check that finds a fact still true is recorded with verified: edit_engram with set_frontmatter and that key stamps who checked and when. It never removes; there is one entry per verifier, refreshed each time that person re-checks. A stale_after pushed to a later date says the same thing with a deadline attached, and the sweep prefers one, since an old engram with no review date and no verification is one it will start asking about on age alone.

An acknowledgment says something different: this finding is intentional. The key evolve_ack with a value naming the rule and an optional note, V101 lineage citation, keep, records that the person ruled on it. The server records what evidence the finding fired on, so the acknowledgment holds exactly as long as that evidence does and comes back marked stale when the evidence changes. Every run reports how many findings acknowledgments suppressed, include_acknowledged shows them with the note on each, and the value remove V101 takes one back. Acknowledge only what the person actually ruled on. The sweep is only as trustworthy as the decisions it was told to respect.

12.7 When to sweep

The sweep is event-driven maintenance, never a session-start ritual. The events that call for it are a large ingest that lands a dozen engrams at once and two search hits that disagree when a half-finished retirement is the likely reason. The third is the calendar: the turn of a month, the first session being the playbook’s suggestion. The reminder at the end of a session helps with the last: when maintenance is due, chapter 8’s stop hook adds a paragraph asking the agent to call evolve_engrams and work the queue, mechanical findings directly, judgment findings one at a time with a yes.

TipUnder the hood

The sweep reads dates, links and graph shape, and chapter 3 was honest about what that excludes. The rule for old, never-verified knowledge is capped per run, oldest first, so an archive that has never been swept is asked about in instalments. And an acknowledgment lives in the engram’s own frontmatter, beside the scope the server recorded for it, so it travels with the file and survives a rebuild of the index, which stores nothing about a sweep between runs.

A colleague who is corrected gets better. A colleague who is never corrected gets confident. The sweep is how you keep telling the difference.

NoteIn brief

Knowledge drifts rather than breaks: windows lapse, review dates pass, replacements land without the retirement being finished, captures go unreviewed, links dangle, tags fork. The sweep, crystalline evolve or evolve_engrams, returns a read-only ranked queue in three families, each finding naming the engram, the evidence and the one action; the agent completes mechanical findings in a pass and proposes judgment findings one at a time, and Fluid’s maintenance page shows a person the same queue, for a look at the state of things rather than for working it. Retire by superseding: a new engram with supersedes, the old one set to superseded with a real valid_to and a superseded_by, and the lesson carried forward; correct in place only when the old fact was never true. A re-check is a verified stamp or a later stale_after; an intentional finding is an evolve_ack naming the rule, which holds while its evidence holds. Sweep after an ingest, when hits disagree and on a cadence, and stop when the number stops falling.
