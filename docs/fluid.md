# Fluid, the web UI

The agent is the primary author; Fluid is where a person reads, corrects and adds, in the browser, without spending a token.

The agent captures and refines engrams as it works, and Fluid is how you take part directly, without going through the LLM. What you write does not sit unreviewed: the agent's next maintenance pass verifies it, aligns the tags and wires it into the graph. Fluid is built into the binary and on by default at `http://localhost:7411`, so the daemon your agents already talk to serves people and agents on one port: nothing to deploy, and the first visit creates your admin account right in the browser.

- **Read what was learned.** An engram is a page: frontmatter as a details rail, observations and relations as labelled chips, backlinks, and the `crystalline://` address one click from the clipboard. Domains down the side, Cmd+K (Ctrl+K off a Mac) to jump anywhere by name, and a full width toggle (`\`) that folds the details rail away and gives the prose the page's full column.
- **Open a domain on its Reflection.** A domain opens on its Reflection, the documents for people, when the Reflection is on and you may read it. The switch at the top reads "Reflection | Engrams", so the engrams are one click away, at `/d/<domain>/engrams`. A domain without a Reflection opens on its engrams as before, and so does one whose Reflection is not in effect here yet or lives on another instance. See [The Reflection](reflection.md).
- **Keep a Reflection page current.** Under a page's title, a few lines say what the page follows and how fresh it is: "Follows: PACT.md > Architecture overview" with each step a link, "Part of: Architecture overview" for a page another page owns, and the clock, for example "Last checked 2 hours ago, due again in 1 hour". A page one whole period past due reads "Overdue since 3 hours, last checked 5 hours ago" in the warning colour, with "The reflect loop runs less often than this page's freshness." when the runs simply come too seldom. A page a reflect run left says "A reflect run skipped this page.", and a due page open in the editor says "Waiting: open in the editor". The lines age on their own while the tab stays open. Beside the page on a wide screen, and under it on a narrow one, the Page details column starts with Page settings. A curator picks the freshness (None, 1 hour, 6 hours, 1 day, 7 days, 30 days or Custom) and the page it follows, and saves them with Save settings. A custom freshness is a number and a unit: h hours, d days, w weeks, m months, y years. `m` means months, not minutes, a bare number is days, and the least is 1 hour. The column also opens the page pact, or creates it for a curator, and offers Retire, Restore (also "Restore under" the former owner) and a two-step Delete. Everyone else reads the settings there without a control. The editor never shows `freshness`, `follows` or `status`. A retired page shows a Retired banner, "Retired: no longer part of Architecture overview" when another page owned it, with restore and delete for a curator. The navigation hides retired pages until you turn on "Show retired pages", a switch that is offered while a page is retired and remembered in this browser, and the Reflection search shows a retired page faded, with the word "retired". An admin sees a banner when a reflect run missed pages ("A reflect run missed 2 Reflection pages."), and the Maintenance screen lists them under "Reflection pages a reflect run missed". See [Pages that keep themselves current](reflection.md#pages-that-keep-themselves-current).
- **Edit in place.** A live-preview markdown editor with table editing, a frontmatter form, mermaid previews, flowcharts you edit by their boxes and arrows, and wikilink completion across every domain. The file on disk stays the source of truth.
- **Attach what you teach with.** Paste, drag or upload a screenshot, a diagram, a slide deck, a PDF or a data file straight onto an engram; agents read those attachments back over MCP, can add or replace one themselves with `write_engram` and a `path` under `assets/`, and evolve keeps the knowledge extracted from them current. An attachment always belongs to some engram's teaching, and a file nothing references is flagged for cleanup rather than left to accumulate. Image references take an optional formatting fragment (a single `#` carrying comma-separated options, `left`, `right`, `center`, `full` and `w=50%` or `w=320`, as in `![Chart](assets/chart.png#right,w=50%)`) that Fluid honors and every other markdown renderer simply ignores.
- **Shape the sidebar.** Drag the divider between the domain sidebar and the page to set its width, from 200 to 480 pixels; double click it to go back to the default. Dragging it under the minimum folds the sidebar to the rail. The divider takes the pointer only (mouse, pen or touch) and is not there in the phone layout, where the sidebar is a drawer. The width is kept in this browser only. After a domain's name the sidebar shows a lock for a private domain and a book for a domain with a Reflection you may read, each with a tooltip and a name for screen readers.
- **Read what is current.** A page you are reading follows the knowledge: when an agent, a colleague or a pull changes the engram, the text updates in place and a line says who changed it. The sidebar and the folder tree follow too. All tabs of one browser share a single connection for this, so it works with any number of tabs, also over plain HTTP/1.1; an HTTP/2 proxy lifts the browser's per-host connection limit anyway.
- **Collaborate in real time.** Everyone in the same engram sees everyone else's cursors and edits live. Changes merge conflict-free and land as one save.
- **Search it all.** Faceted search across the whole instance, backed by the same hybrid text-plus-semantic ranking the agents use.
- **See what the knowledge needs next.** A maintenance page with the ranked queue of everything due (stale dates, half-finished retirements, unreviewed human captures), the same queue the agent works.
- **Take part in the team loop.** A team domain shows its proposals in the browser: where each one stands, what the review said, a share dialog for the changes waiting to go out and a withdraw that closes a proposal on GitHub (optionally restoring the files it shared). A conflict opens a dialog with both sides next to each other, so keeping yours, taking theirs or hand-merging is a decision you make with the wording in front of you.
- **See what is locked.** A read-only domain wears a lock on its page and on every engram page, with the line "its owner switches read_only off in the domain policies". Its owner or an instance admin switches the lock in the domain policies card, on or off. Turning it on asks first, and nobody else is offered the switch. While the lock holds, the `read_only` row is the only row in the card that can change. Where you cannot write for a reason of your own (a viewer role, or a write list that leaves the domain out), Fluid shows no lock and offers no edit, new, move or delete there.
- **See the shape of it.** An interactive graph of any engram's neighborhood, and an agent's-eye view showing exactly what the tools serve an agent for that page.
- **Look after the instance in one place.** An admin finds everything about the instance under Administration at `/admin` (also in the avatar menu): General with the version, a newer release when the server's update check found one, and the address, then Users, Agents, Invites and the GitHub connection, each a page of the side navigation (a select on a narrow screen). Maintenance stays at `/maintenance`, because every role may open it, and the area links to it. Nobody else sees the area: every address under `/admin` answers them as a mistyped one does. The old addresses `/users` and `/settings/github` still work and lead on to `/admin/users` and `/admin/github`, keeping the query and the base path.
- **Find one account among hundreds.** The Users page is a table of name, display name, role, state, sign-in (password, SSO, proxy or token only) and last seen. Search by name, display name or a whole email address, filter by role, state, sign-in, GitHub link and the days an account was last seen (from and until, both days included), sort by any column and page through 25, 50 or 100 at a time. All of it is in the address, so a list can be linked, and the back button walks through it. Sorting by last seen can move an account to another page while you page, and the table says so. A row opens the account at `/admin/users/<name>`, beside the table on a wide screen and as its own page on a narrow one, with the focus on its name; closing it puts the focus back on the row. There you change the display name, role, Reflection level, state, write list (the domains it may write to, as for `crystalline users writes`), password and GitHub link, and delete the account (asked twice). Its drafts are counted per domain but not changed: they belong to the person. A deleted account's drafts are ended from the list. Tick rows on one page to deactivate, reactivate or change the role of all of them at once: Fluid asks first with the count, and then lists every account with what happened, refusals first. Your own account cannot be selected, and its page offers no deactivate.
- **Give an agent of the loops its tokens.** The Agents page lists every agent with its display name, its loops (reflect, evolve or both) and its tokens, each with its label, when it ends and when it was last used. A live token that ends within 14 days is marked. Add an agent with its name, display name, loops and first token, or issue another token for an agent (label and life in days, 1 to 365, 90 by default). A token is shown once in a field with a copy button and is gone after Done or when you leave the page. Two tokens can be live at once, so a new one can start before the old one is revoked; the server refuses a third and Fluid shows its sentence. Revoking a token and removing an agent are asked twice, and removing an agent also discards its drafts. Agents work only with `auth.mcp` on: while it is off the page says so at the top and the add button is held back with that reason. After a change of `auth.mcp`, restart the server. The page shows no runs: run records are kept per domain, and a domain's automation card shows them.
- **Invite someone new with a link.** The Invites page makes a link for any role below admin, optionally with a private domain and the level it gives there, a label and a life of 1 to 30 days (7 by default). On an instance where single sign-on or a proxy names people, a link can also allow a local account with a password; elsewhere every link makes a local account anyway, so the choice is not offered. The link is shown once with a copy button and is gone after Done or when you leave the page; hand it over yourself, nothing is mailed. Without `service.public_url` Fluid completes the link with the address this browser opened it at, keeping the base path. The token sits after `#`, so it never reaches a log. The page lists pending and used links, and revoked or expired ones under Show; a pending link is revoked after a second press. Only admins open this page; a domain's managers make their links from its Members card.
- **Add people by name.** On a private domain's Members card, the Account field finds people as you type: from two letters it searches login and display name and lists up to ten, each by display name and login. Someone who is already a member, or the owner, stays in the list with that reason and cannot be picked. Transfer ownership uses the same field. When nobody matches and you may make invite links here, "Invite someone new" opens a link form for the domain: the level (viewer, editor or manager), a label and the days it lives. Such a link gives instance role `none`, so the person sees only the domains they are invited into. If a search fails, the field says why, for example when there were too many searches in a minute. Admins may always make these links; owners and managers only when the instance allows it (`auth.invites.managers`). The link is shown once with a copy button, and the card lists your own pending links to the domain with a revoke for each. A domain whose access follows GitHub takes its people from GitHub, so it has no picker and no links.
- **Take an invite link.** A link opens the invite page at `/invite`. The page reads the token from the part after `#`, takes it off the address bar at once and sends it only in the body of a request. It shows who made the link, the domain, the level and when the link ends, and it takes nothing until you press a button. Then it offers the way in the instance names: choose a login and a password (or sign in to the account you have here) on an instance with local accounts, one button for single sign-on that brings you back signed in on the domain's page, or, when you are signed in already, one button to accept for your account. A link that was used, revoked, has expired or can no longer work says "This invite link is not valid any more."
- **Accounts when you need them, none when you don't.** Admin, editor and viewer roles managed in the UI or with `crystalline users`; an anonymous read-only mode for a published archive; a trusted-header mode behind an SSO proxy. See [deployment](deployment.md) for the container and team-server variants.
